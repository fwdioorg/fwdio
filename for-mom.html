What's Really Cookin'?
The lump sum isn't free money — it's a trade. Taking it just tells Social Security "pretend I filed six months ago." They hand over those six months as a check… and then lock the monthly payment in a little lower, for good. So the real choice is: a pile of cash today, or a fatter check every month for life.
The Break-Even Race
Cash now: $29,000. Bigger check: $203/month.
$29,000 ÷ $203 ≈ 12 years — right around age 82 (a touch sooner once yearly raises kick in). Past that line, Option B pulls ahead and keeps winning every month after.
Live past 82?  Option B wins.

Myth Buster
THE MYTH: "Taxes will hit you retroactively!"
THE TRUTH: Nope. The lump is taxed the year it's received — no reopening old returns — and there's even an IRS option that can lower the tax. At a modest income it barely matters. Don't let confusing tax talk decide this.
Before You Sign — Ask the Waiter
Have Social Security spell out exactly which six months the lump covers. Any of those months that land after the 70th birthday cost nothing in credits — so the real "price" may be even smaller than $203. Get it in writing before deciding.
The Chef's Recommendation
Take the $29,000 only if you truly want the cash in hand — a real cushion or a real plan for it.
If it would just get parked and invested, skip it and keep the bigger check. That check already beats what the money could safely earn — and it protects her most in her 90s.
